# Expert Interview Protocol

**Study:** *Shadows of Transparency: Signaling Transparency-Impeding Behavior Using Public Data*
**Duration:** ± 60 minutes
**Format:** Semi-structured interview
**Versions:** Journalists · Researchers (transparency expert) · Researchers (data specialist) · Civil servants

> *Note.* This is an English translation of the original Dutch instrument used in the
> study. In the paper these behaviors are called *behavioral patterns*; the instrument
> below uses the equivalent term *anti-patterns*. "Woo" refers to the current Dutch FOI
> Act (Wet open overheid).

---

## General Introduction (± 5 min)

**Introduction.** Thank you for taking part in this research. This interview is part of a study. The aim is to gain insight into behaviors that can impede transparency within the Woo procedure. We have developed a framework of nine recurring anti-patterns, i.e., behaviors that systematically limit transparency, and translated them into measurable indicators based on Woo response data.

**Consent.**
- May I record this interview for transcription and analysis? The recording remains confidential and is used solely for research purposes.
- You may stop the interview or skip any question at any time.
- Your answers will be anonymized.

---

## Presentation (± 10 min)

*[Show the overview of the three categories and nine patterns, with tables and examples.]*

**Categories of anti-patterns**

- **1 Reactive Document Management:** behaviors that determine which documents exist or are findable.
  *Renamed Units · Document Suppression · Reclassification*
- **2 Interpretative Manipulation:** formal responses that limit factual openness.
  *Narrow Reading · Overbroad Reading · Catch-All Exemption*
- **3 Disclosure Manipulation:** release of information in hard-to-use forms.
  *Excessive Redaction · Obstructive Formatting*

*Stale Release (delaying disclosure until its value diminishes) was presented as an additional, timing-related pattern alongside the three categories.*

**Explanation shown to participants.**
- *Table 1:* the categories and patterns.
- *Table 2:* links patterns to indicators such as redaction rate, FAIRI-score, time to decision, distribution of disclosed documents, and frequency of exemption grounds.
- *Example findings:* differences in redaction rates across ministries, FAIRIscore distribution, response-time trends.

## 1 Journalists

### Experience with practice (10 min)
1. Do you recognize the described anti-patterns from your experience with Woo requests?
   - Which patterns do you encounter most often?
   - Are there patterns you do not recognize or find implausible?
2. Are there other behaviors that, in your view, impede transparency but are not in our framework?
   - Can you give a concrete example?

### Validation of indicators (15 min)
3. Do you think our indicators measure what they intend to measure?
   - E.g., does "average redaction rate" give a good picture of excessive redaction?
   - Are some indicators stronger or weaker than others?
4. When you see that "Ministry X has an average redaction rate of 45%, against 18% on average," how do you interpret that?
   - Would you read it as a sign of obstruction, or could there be legitimate explanations?
   - What context do you need to interpret it?

### Strengths and limitations of using the data (15 min)
5. What do you see as the main advantages of using Woo data to analyze transparency behavior?
6. What do you see as the biggest limitations of this approach?
   - Does analyzing only released documents affect the conclusions?
   - What forms of bias can occur?
7. How credible do you find these indicators as evidence of organizational behavior?
   - What would increase your confidence?
   - What threshold or trend would convince you of an anti-pattern?

### Alternative explanations and context (10 min)
8. What alternative explanations could there be for the patterns we measure, besides deliberate obstruction?
   - E.g., staffing capacity, legal necessity, complexity of requests.
9. What do you consider a "normal" or acceptable level of redaction or processing time?
   - Would comparison with other countries, years, or ministries help interpretation?

### Application and future (10 min)
10. How could you, as a journalist, use these indicators or findings in your work?
    - Would they influence your investigative priorities?
11. Suppose a particular agency shows striking scores — how would you investigate this further?
    - What additional information would you seek?
12. What information or data would make these analyses more reliable or useful?
13. How do you see the future of data-driven transparency research?

### Closing (5 min)
14. Is there anything we have not discussed that is important for understanding transparency-impeding behavior?
15. Are there aspects of our method or results you would like to discuss further?

## 2. Researcher (Transparency Expert)

### Recognition and completeness (10 min)
1. Do you recognize the anti-patterns from public-administration theory or practice?
   - Which patterns do you find most representative of structural transparency problems?
2. Are there behaviors or institutional mechanisms our inventory does not capture?

### Theoretical validity (15 min)
3. Do you think the framework operationalizes the conceptual essence of "transparency impediment" well?
4. Are the patterns better understood as intentional behavior, organizational effect, or both?
5. Which theoretical perspectives do you find relevant for this analysis? How would you situate it across micro, meso, and macro scales?

### Interpretation and context (15 min)
6. How do legal, political, and organizational contexts influence the occurrence of these patterns?
7. When is a high redaction or delay trend a sign of obstruction, and when is it not?
8. What qualitative information is needed to interpret the quantitative indicators?

### Method and credibility (10 min)
9. How reliable do you consider FOI response data as a source for inferring organizational behavior?
10. Which methodological improvements would increase the credibility of our claims?
    - E.g., additional indicators, triangulation, weighting, or clustering.
11. Which benchmarks or comparisons would aid interpretation (international, historical, inter-departmental)?

### Value, application, and future (10 min)
12. What role do you see for this type of data-driven analysis within transparency research?
13. How can quantitative patterns and qualitative public-administration insights reinforce each other?
14. What is the greatest added value, or the greatest risk, of an anti-pattern approach?
15. What would be needed to turn this kind of analysis into a standard transparency-measurement instrument?

### Closing (5 min)
16. Are there themes we have not discussed that you consider essential for understanding transparency impediment?
17. Are there specific aspects of our method or findings you would like to reflect on further?

## 3. Researcher (Data Specialist / Dataset Builder)

### Dataset structure and representativeness (10 min)
1. Can you briefly explain how the dataset was built (sources, scraping, filtering)?
2. Are certain types of Woo responses over- or under-represented?
3. Are there variables or metadata missing that would be useful for our analysis?
4. Is this set representative of published Woo decisions?

### Indicator construction and validity (15 min)
5. How do you assess the technical reliability of our indicators (redaction rate, FAIRI-score, processing time)?
6. What forms of measurement error or bias can occur in automatic extraction?
7. What normalization or standardization is required to compare organizations fairly?

### Limitations and improvements (15 min)
8. What are the biggest limitations of this dataset for behavioral analysis?
9. How could the dataset be extended or enriched (e.g., with information on requesters, legal categories, or internal logs)? Is that technically feasible?
10. How can we handle missing values without introducing bias?

### Interpretation and context (10 min)
11. How should users interpret context-sensitive indicators such as the redaction rate?
12. Which technical limitations must be made explicit to non-specialists (journalists, policymakers)?

### Closing (5 min)
13. Is there anything we have not discussed that is important for further developing this type of dataset?
14. Are there parts of our approach you would like to discuss or give feedback on?

## 4. Civil Servants

### Recognition and completeness of patterns (15 min)
1. Do you recognize the described anti-patterns as behaviors that sometimes occur?
   - Which do you recognize most?
   - Which least?
2. Are there other behaviors that are not mentioned?

### Indicators and credibility (15 min)
3. How realistic do you find our indicators as measures of transparency?
   - Which aspects do they measure well, which less so?
4. What context is needed to interpret the figures correctly?
5. What would increase your confidence that these data give a fair picture?
6. What additional data could we use to put these figures in perspective or to validate them?

### Comparison (10 min)
7. Which comparisons (between organizations, years, countries) would help put the results in perspective?
8. What does a comparison between, for example, departments or ministries tell us?

### Application and future (10 min)
9. How could your organization use these indicators to improve processes?
10. What would be needed to use this analysis as a standard tool?

### Closing (5 min)
11. Is there anything we have not discussed that is important for understanding transparency-impeding behavior in practice?
